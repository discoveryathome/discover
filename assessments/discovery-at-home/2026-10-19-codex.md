Suggestions that do not change these grades:

1. Implement issue-transport board support (#27), including `responds_to` lifecycle fixtures and a visible index of open handoffs.
2. Name who reviews the declared 5,000 contributions/week, preserve review outcomes, and distinguish independent review from maintainer self-merges.
3. Preserve/regenerate adapters, exact probe inputs, transcripts and model identifiers; rerun the four unrerun adapters with fresh controls and repetitions.
4. Reaffirm priorities after the frontier update, documenting whether its existing bottleneck/weights still hold. If this assessment is adopted, update the frontier and registry consistently.

Problems encountered in the playbook/rubric:

- **Two handoffs on one track do not exist.** There are two total, on different tracks. I checked both and used two open tooling requests for the same-track probe, following spec §3.2's broader open-request wording. The playbook should define the zero/one-handoff case rather than require inventing work.
- **Unattended execution vs opt-in.** The hard rule requires explicit permission before executing a declared check, while §3 expects it to run. With no person available, I kept the assessment by inspection. The playbook needs a standard way to supply execution permission up front and distinguish a policy-limited probe from an intrinsically unreplayable result. No automatic approval rejection occurred.
- **Probe standard is not operationally pinned.** No v0 donor-tier model or maintainer-approved reference answers were supplied. A fresh smaller-model probe was possible, but its timing/model label is not directly comparable with the old Haiku probes; the saved inputs/answers should accompany the grade.
- **Coverage vocabulary is theorem-shaped.** `computation` is the closest allowed label for structural record consistency; it must not imply that assessment arguments or historical adapter comparisons were reproduced.
- **Steering clock and capacity fallback need judgment rules.** Evidence-packaging corrections can reset the frontier clock without changing useful priorities, and an uncorroborated numeric capacity can unlock Stage 1. Both current rules were applied as written; neither was silently repaired during grading.
- **Re-assessment vs exact historical rerun.** The requested current-main re-assessment is not a same-commit replay of the 2026-10-17 record. Comparison with PR #31 is stronger because its assessed tree differs only in site presentation. The record should say which sort of independence it establishes.
