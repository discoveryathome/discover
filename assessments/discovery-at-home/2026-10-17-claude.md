**Stage: assessed.** Consent and a credit policy are present, but Stage 0 needs Orientation at least adequate, and it is weak. Stage 1 also needs Orientation strong, Verification at least adequate and Review at least adequate (declared capacity would do).

What blocks the next stage, each fixable in an afternoon:

1. **Stage 0:** update `discover/frontier.json` and DISCOVERY.md "Current state" to what the repo shows (the record format exists; applied-butterfly-mult is assessed; integer-mult-bounds' Stage 1 is a smoke test without a record), and mark #3 (and #1) with what's done and what's left.
2. **Stage 1, Verification:** back each frontier claim with a checkable record (an assessment file per graded project, or drop the integer-mult-bounds Stage 1 claim), and name the check for it, for example an independent rerun of the probe at the recorded commit.
3. **Stage 1, Review:** declare review capacity (who reviews hub PRs, how many a week, typical response time).

## Suggestions (don't affect grades)

1. **Maintainer handle.** `discover.json` `maintainers[0].github` and the registry `contact` are `colkitt`, which isn't a GitHub account (`gh api users/colkitt` → 404). The account acting as maintainer is `Mister-Meeseeks`. The same `contact` is on `integer-mult-bounds.json`. Hub tooling and donors can't reach or verify a maintainer through a handle that doesn't exist.
2. **Coordination transport.** Either enable Discussions and follow the hub's own recommended convention (§3.5), or teach `discover board`/`post` to read issues. As it stands the hub's own tools can't see the hub's own claims.
3. **Claiming rule.** DISCOVERY.md:46 and the card issues ("Sessions under an hour may skip claiming") contradict `contribute.md`, which since `579291b` says claim unless the task takes under five minutes.
4. **Stale positive controls.** The card issues expect `discover 0.1.0`; the CLI prints `discover 0.2.0`. The catalog's `expected` ("a line starting with discover") is fine; regenerate the issue bodies.
5. **`featured: true` on integer-mult-bounds** with no assessment or stage in its entry: a featured listing should have a record behind it (§3.3 reserves featuring for Stage 2).
6. **Self-merge.** Consider a rule that hub changes affecting grades or the rubric go through a PR that someone other than the author reviews (a donated `verify-assessments` session would do).

## Doesn't fit the rubric

- **Grading against a stale record.** §3.2 grades Orientation against "the project's own record of what's accepted". Here the record *understates* the repo: the probe's answers match the record and are wrong about the project. I graded weak because the staleness would misdirect work (exactly what "adequate" rules out), but the rubric doesn't say which reference wins when the record and the repo disagree. A sentence in §3.2 would settle it.
- **A hub grading itself.** Nothing in §3, §11 or §12 says who may assess the hub's own listing, or that consent from the hub's own `discover.json` plus approval by the same maintainer is self-consent. Suggest: the hub's own assessment needs an independent rerun before its stage is shown, or is labeled self-assessed.
- **Intake test traffic as contributions.** Every PR and issue in the window came from the maintainer exercising the intake pipeline. The rubric counts "contributions opened in the window" without saying whether a maintainer's own or bot-generated PRs count; I excluded them as not contributions awaiting review.
- **`review-only` verification profile.** For a project whose results are judgments (grades), there's no runnable `reproduce`. The natural check is an independent rerun of a probe, which §3.2 Verification's "declared check the probe completes" doesn't quite describe. The rubric could name "independent rerun at the recorded commit" as the check for review-only projects.
- **Standing cards vs. handoffs.** The hub's issues are standing cards that stay open across many contributions (#1 had a result and stayed open). The live-state probe asks about handoffs; it doesn't say how to treat a card that's partly done.
- **Probe timing.** The probe reported about one minute of reading by its own timestamps (about 1.5 minutes elapsed). The 15-minute budget is far from binding for a repo this size, so "within 15 minutes" doesn't discriminate here.
- **Probe error to note for reliability work:** it read the applied-butterfly-mult record's "maintainer-reviewed 2026-10-17" (about that project's frontier) as a claim about the hub's frontier and reported a contradiction that isn't one.

Notes on this run: the playbook asks to confirm before running code; it ran unattended, so I ran only the declared `make test` in a disposable clone (`~/discover-work/assess-discoveryathome-discover/repo`, 1.6 MB with logs) and nothing else. No posts, issues or PRs.
