### 1. Orientation: strong

The README answers the four questions in its first screens: the problem (smallest container
for n congruent copies of a convex solid, with exact certificates), what's established and
what isn't ("Rigor"; `docs/MATH.md`: every entry is an upper bound, never optimality), where
results live (`records/`, the two `SUMMARY` CSVs, `records/higher.md`), and how to contribute
(`CONTRIBUTING.md`: the contribution page, the `submit-packing` form, the automatic check, the
acceptance rule "strictly below the current certified coordinate record by more than 1e-12").
`docs/MATH.md` and `docs/FINAL_REPORT.md` give the method and history in depth.

Two things a newcomer has to work around, both handled by the adapter: there's no agent entry
file or task list, and `STATUS.md` (13 Oct) says the search was stopped, while the README
describes the 15 Oct expansions. The hub's 2026-10-19 evaluation of this commit graded
Orientation strong with the adapter; nothing in the repository has changed since.

### 2. Coordination: weak

- **Spread: adequate, with the adapter.** The project has no task list or picker. The adapter
  supplies a seeded draw over the enumerated 3D cases (the two `SUMMARY` CSVs), labelled as the
  hub's reading. It doesn't see pending submissions, so it's "a draw that ignores live
  claims". Duplicate rate: unknown. Outside work in the window is three accounts, nearly all
  from one, in non-overlapping batches.
- **Live state: weak.** There's no claim mechanism. Pending work is visible only as open
  submission issues, which say what was submitted but not what anyone is working on. Telling
  open from taken cases means searching issues by case id.

The axis takes the lower half: weak.

### 3. Verification: strong

- **One authoritative record:** a record file per (problem, n) under `records/`, with its
  checker outputs (`.certify.txt`, `.verify.txt`) beside it, plus the community and external
  registries. `comparison_records()` in `scripts/check_submission.py` defines the baseline a
  submission must beat from all of them.
- **A per-row check that recomputes the object:** `certify_general.py RECORD.json` proves, in
  exact arithmetic, that every piece is congruent to the canonical solid, inside the container,
  and separated from every other piece by an explicit plane. It needs only the standard
  library and runs in well under a second for n = 40 (README, "Rigor"; `CONTRIBUTING.md`).
  `certify_exact.py` covers the Platonic records and `certify_nd.py` the 4D–6D ones.
- **It demonstrably runs, independently of the submitter:** the submission workflow
  (`.github/workflows/submission.yml`) runs the float and exact checks on every candidate in
  CI and posts the logs. Issue #98 shows 37 candidates certified that way on 2026-10-18,
  with the full exact-certificate output.
- **Formal evidence:** Lean checks of 532 frozen witnesses across all 140 families
  (`docs/research-2026-10-16-generalization/`), scoped as feasibility of those witnesses.
- **Coverage:** each entry claims feasibility (an upper bound), and the exact certificate
  checks exactly that. Optimality is stated as not proved.

**The checks this assessment would run** (not run, see above): `python3 certify_general.py
records/cubincub/cubincub_n12.json` and the same on three rows drawn from the CSVs;
`python3 certify_exact.py records/tetintet/tetintet_n07.json`; `python3 certify_nd.py` on one
`records/higher.md` witness; and `bash test/run_all.sh`, which needs Node ≥ 18 and Python with
NumPy and SciPy. A rerun should confirm these before the grade is relied on for a higher stage.

### 4. Review: adequate

Contributions here arrive as submission issues, not PRs: every PR in the window is the
maintainer's own. Measured on outside submission issues (81 in the window, from BuddenPing,
kanin-design and konoha-ily2), counting the maintainer's comment, label or close as the first
response:

- **Median first response:** under an hour (most of the window's batches were accepted and
  closed the same day, 15 Oct).
- **Waited more than 7 days:** 1 of the 4 submissions old enough to tell (25%). With fewer than
  20, the share is unknown, so the grade is at most adequate.
- **Backlog:** grew from 1 to 7 between the window's midpoint and its end. The maintainer's
  last activity is 2026-10-16. Waiting now: #98 (37 certified `cubincub` improvements, 18 Oct,
  only the bot's check), #1 (three `cubincub` improvements, 11 Oct), the 4D–6D constructions
  #28, #79 and #85 (15 Oct, which need the maintainer to dispatch the nD workflow), and #12 and
  #35, labelled `accepted` but still open.

The submission bot posts an exact verdict within minutes, but acceptance needs a maintainer's
label, and the project doesn't declare mechanical acceptance, so the bot's verdict isn't
counted as review. If the backlog keeps growing, Review falls to weak.

**Rubric gap:** the rubric counts PRs as contributions. Here they're issues; this assessment
measured issues instead and says so.

### 5. Steering: adequate

Open directions are stated in `docs/MATH.md` §8 and `docs/FINAL_REPORT.md` §9.2 (11 Oct), and
the search scope in the README. They're dated and partly overtaken (the README's later
expansions; other Platonic pairs now done), and nothing states priorities for outside
contributors. Adequate: stated, but stale.

## What the listing should promise

Certified records in the less-searched cases of a large catalogue (n = 41–100 and the newer
families), each checked mechanically; reproducing and certifying known records elsewhere.
Not new records on the well-studied Platonic cases with n ≤ 40 in a session. Improvements in
cases first filled by nested constructions often beat a placeholder rather than the best known
value (#98's n = 82–100 are at the 5×5×5 grid), so a session should compare with the published
values in `docs/external_records.json` before calling one a record. The bottleneck is search;
acceptance waits on the maintainer.
