### 1. Orientation: adequate (by reading)

The entry chain answers all four questions. `AGENTS.md` sends agents to `DISCOVERY.md`,
`MODEL.md` and `LITERATURE.md`, states the permissions, the check and the verify
command, and says to use the project's own seeded picker.

- **Problem:** exact maximum-parsimony scoring and search for 17 fixed nucleotide
  alignments, under one scoring profile (`unordered-unit-strict-v1`), with no biological
  truth claim (`README.md`, `DISCOVERY.md`, `MODEL.md`).
- **Current result:** `discover/frontier.json` and `discover/frontier-rows.json`, one row
  per alignment, eight settled and nine open; the README's table matches.
  `pythonidae-16s-12` stands at 95 in the frontier, while a donor-trial candidate at 93
  (`results/donor-trial-001/REPORT.md`) is a pending proposal. `README.md` and
  `DISCOVERY.md` say so and warn not to count 93 as new. A careful reader gets this
  right; a hurried one might not.
- **Where help is wanted:** `discover/catalog.json` (five tracks) and
  `discover/priority-log.md`, drawn by `scripts/pick.py`.
- **How to contribute:** `AGENTS.md` and `DISCOVERY.md`: work locally by default, verify
  with `phylo.py verify`, keep seeds, budgets and verifier output, and end messages with a
  `discover` block.

Strong needs the probe's timing. The docs look strong-ready.

### 3. Verification: strong (covers: computation)

- **One authoritative record:** `discover/frontier.json`, a record table with a
  per-row check (`python3 phylo.py verify {dataset} {tree} --claim {score}`), evidence
  level `author-checked`, claim kind `exact`.
- **The declared check, run:** `python3 scripts/check.py` (Python 3.14, standard
  library only, offline) passed in 1 minute 45 seconds, against a stated 3 minutes. It
  replays the data curation byte for byte, checks source hashes, recomputes every row's
  score from its witness tree, checks baseline and published scores, re-enumerates the
  exhaustive optima, verifies the incompatible-pair lower-bound certificates, enforces
  `settled` exactly when the bound meets the score, and runs 16 unit tests.
- **Row check, spot-run:** `phylo.py verify pythonidae-16s-12
  results/donor-trial-001/spr-plateau.nwk --claim 93` confirms 93 in 0.14 s; `--claim
  92` is rejected.
- **Coverage:** the claims are exact scores of witnessed trees and certified lower
  bounds, and the check recomputes both. So it covers what the record claims.
- **Packaging:** results carry tree hashes, alignment SHA-256, seeds, budgets and
  replay data (`scripts/replay_search.py`; the donor trial's `submission.json` and
  receipts). The only contribution so far, the trial, follows them.

What it doesn't show: the frontier is compared with the project's own starting trees,
and with published scores only for the two PHYLIP tutorials. The empirical rows are
derived subsets with no published optimum, and **no mature parsimony program (TNT,
PAUP*, MPBoot) has been run on the identical inputs**. The project lists this as its
next depth (`HANDOFF.md`). For 12–29 taxa, such programs often find, and for the smaller
rows prove by branch and bound, the optimum in seconds. So the open rows' gaps may be
much easier to close than they look.
