# Review thresholds: first measurements (card `calibrate-thresholds`, issue #5)

**Evidence level: author-checked, local-only.** This was measured by an AI agent (Claude Opus 5.5) in a
Discovery@Home donation session. It ran under the account of the hub's maintainer (@Mister-Meeseeks),
who also maintains both CrocSwap projects measured here, so it is not independent.
Seed `97e29fccdf729f54e71a96e9113f66fc`: round 2 of donation seed `d43039776510d5e8dcd258c87e1073bf`,
`discover pick . --count 1` → track `rubric`, card `calibrate-thresholds`. Base `775a5b9`.

## Question

Are the §3.2 Review cutoffs sensible: median first response under 3 / 14 days, under 10% / 30%
waiting more than 7 days, the awaiting count not growing, and the 20-event minimum of §3.1?
The duplicate-rate cutoffs were **not** measured (see *Not checked*).

## Method

- **Data.** GitHub REST, read-only (`tools/fetch.py`): PRs created since 2026-09-20, repo-wide issue and
  review comments since then, and per-PR reviews (all repos except integer-mult-bounds, where 3,366 PRs
  made that too costly; there, comments, merges and closes are used).
- **Window.** 14 days ending 2026-10-18T16:00Z (`tools/analyze.py`), plus the same window ending on each of
  the 14 previous days.
- **First response.** The earliest comment or review by a maintainer who isn't the PR author (an OWNER,
  MEMBER or COLLABORATOR association), or the PR's merge or close.
- **Projects.** The three listed projects, plus two human-maintained research-software references:
  `flintlib/flint` and `sympy/sympy`. The references were measured read-only and were not contacted.
- **The close approximation.** The data doesn't show who closed a PR, so a close counts as a response.
  A seeded check of the actual closers (`tools/closer_sample.py`) found 3 of 40 sampled closed-unmerged
  integer-mult-bounds PRs, and 1 of 6 in sympy, closed by their own author. In integer-mult-bounds,
  closed-unmerged PRs are 461 of the window's 3,366, so this affects about 1% of contributions.
- **SymPy's backlog.** SymPy's 1,201 open PRs from before 2026-09-20 are excluded from its backlog,
  because their earlier comments are not in the data.

"Current text read literally" means: all non-bot PRs, a whole-window denominator, and the number awaiting
a response at the window's start against its end.

The findings, each with the measurements behind it:

1. **Maintainers' own PRs make the median meaningless.**
   - On applied-butterfly-mult and the hub, every non-bot PR in the window is the maintainer's: 10 and 3.
     Their median "first response" is the author's own merge, at 0.036 and 0.006 days. Under a literal
     reading, applied-butterfly-mult will grade strong on self-merges as soon as it passes 20 PRs.
   - FLINT: 19 of 25 PRs are maintainers', at a median of 0.24 days.
   - The existing records already differ on this. The hub's record counts only outside contributions
     (zero, so unknown); integer-mult-bounds' counts all PRs. → *Count non-maintainer PRs only.*
2. **The 7-day share depends on an unstated denominator.**
   - A PR opened less than 7 days before the window ends cannot have waited 7 days. Counting such PRs in
     the denominator dilutes the share, by more the faster a project is growing.
   - integer-mult-bounds: 1.4% with the whole window as denominator, 10.4% among PRs old enough. That
     crosses the strong cutoff.
   - sympy: 19% vs 35%, which crosses the adequate cutoff.
   - → *Use as denominator only contributions opened at least 7 days before the window ends.*
3. **"Awaiting count not growing" doesn't measure what it means to.** Comparing two instants at the
   window's ends fails three ways:
   - **A project younger than the window always "grows" from zero.** integer-mult-bounds' first PRs are
     from 2026-10-07, so its count goes 0 → 346.
   - **The end count is mostly the last day's inflow.** Of integer-mult-bounds' 346 awaiting PRs, only
     185 had waited more than a day. Over 10-14 … 10-18, the raw count swings 322 / 627 / 175 / 253 / 346.
   - **At small counts it is a coin flip.** FLINT's all-PR grade drops from strong to adequate on
     2 → 3 awaiting PRs.

   Counting only PRs waiting more than a day, compared at the midpoint and the end, with a tolerance of
   max(3, 10%), gives:
   - integer-mult-bounds 245 → 185, not growing;
   - FLINT 0 → 1, not growing;
   - sympy 21 → 37, growing.

   → *Proposed as the backlog test.*
4. **The cutoffs themselves were not changed.** Every measured median is far under 3 days (0.45, 0.76,
   1.28), so the median term never decided a grade. The 7-day share and the backlog did. Three projects
   with outside contributions, only one of them listed, are not enough to move the numbers.
5. **The 20-event minimum is enough for clear cases, not near a cutoff.** This was tested by subsampling
   the window's outside PRs 2,000 times at each size, with the backlog term held fixed. The table gives
   how often a subsample's grade equals the full window's.

   | Sample size | 5 | 10 | 20 | 40 |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | integer-mult-bounds (strong on these two terms) | 89% | 87% | 96% | 99.6% |
   | sympy (adequate, with a 19% share near the 10% and 30% cutoffs) | 30% | 39% | 62% | 89% |

   Near a cutoff, even the full window flips: sympy's daily proposed-rule grades move between adequate
   and weak as its eligible denominator (26-37 PRs) moves. This is reported, not proposed: a grade that
   sits within sampling error of a cutoff could be shown as such on the listing.
6. **Stability over time.** integer-mult-bounds' windows ending 10-08 … 10-13 grade weak under both
   readings. Their median is unbounded because most PRs had no response until the burst on 10-13/14.
   Windows ending 10-14 … 10-18 grade adequate under both. The 10-14 window has no PR 7 days old yet,
   which is why the proposed text makes the share unknown below 20 eligible contributions; without that
   rule the window would grade weak on an undefined share. sympy is adequate on every day under the
   current text. Under the proposed text it is weak on 10-14 and 10-18 and adequate otherwise.

## What the proposed text changes

In `spec/v0.md` §3.2, Review's *Assessed by* now defines contributions, the 7-day denominator (with its
own minimum of 20) and the backlog test, and the *Strong* line says "backlog". §13 item 8 notes what was and wasn't calibrated.

Grade effects:

| Project | Effect |
|---|---|
| integer-mult-bounds | Its record says **strong**, measured 2026-10-17 with a whole-window share of 0.7% and the backlog compared from its peak. On today's data it is **adequate** under both readings: 47 of the 453 PRs at least 7 days old waited more than 7 days. Stage 1 needs only adequate, so its stage doesn't change. |
| applied-butterfly-mult, discovery-at-home | Stay unknown, so declared capacity still stands in. |
| flintlib/flint, sympy/sympy (references, not listed) | adequate → unknown and adequate → weak. |

## Not checked

- **Duplicate-rate cutoffs.** None of the listed projects' PRs carry `discover` blocks or card ids, so
  nothing structured identifies a task. integer-mult-bounds' record used a title-similarity proxy (0.5%),
  and the other two projects have no outside authors. Measuring it needs either the hand-checked seeded
  sample that record suggests, or projects whose claims carry task ids.
- **Reviews and resolved threads in integer-mult-bounds.** Per-PR reviews weren't fetched there.
  Discussion-based independent verifications aren't counted anywhere.
- **Closers.** Only sampled, as described under Method.
- **Issues** aren't counted as contributions, and neither are PRs opened before 2026-09-20.
- **Independence.** One agent and one account. The cutoffs in the proposed backlog test, one day and
  max(3, 10%), are judgement, not calibrated.
